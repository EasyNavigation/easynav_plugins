Detailed Description

easynav_mppi_controller

Description

A Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller for Easy Navigation, for differential and holonomic (omnidirectional, mecanum) robots, round or not.

  • G. Williams, A. Aldrich, E. Theodorou, Model Predictive Path Integral Control: From Theory to Parallel Computation. Journal of Guidance, Control, and Dynamics, 2017.

Algorithm

Every cycle, the controller keeps a sequence of horizon_steps velocity commands (dt seconds each), the solution of the previous cycle shifted by the time elapsed since. It simulates num_samples rollouts of the robot:

  • the previous solution, and braking to a stop;
  • deterministic candidates: arcs, turns in place, turning and then going straight (differential), straight lines in eight directions (holonomic), and straight to the goal braking to stop there when the goal is within reach;
  • the rest, the previous solution plus noise correlated in time (noise_correlation), so that the rollouts really turn instead of zigzagging; a tenth of them around zero, to explore.

Each rollout respects the robot's velocity and acceleration limits, and is scored (lower is better):

Term Weight
Distance to the path path_weight From the path pose nearest the robot, as far as the rollouts reach
Path left to the end of that local path progress_weight Rewards going far along the path, soon
Distance to the goal, when within reach goal_weight
Heading error to the heading of heading_mode heading_weight Gives way to the goal's heading within goal_heading_distance of the goal (holonomic)
Heading error to the goal's heading, near it goal_heading_weight Holonomic
Within the safety margin of an obstacle obstacle_weight Quadratic, from safety_radius - radius to touching it
Touching an obstacle collision_cost Per step: such a rollout is discarded
Backward velocity forward_weight Differential
Accelerations smoothness_weight

When an obstacle lies on the path within reach, the path and heading weights drop to a hundredth: the path is only a guide around it. The new solution is the average of the rollouts weighted by exp(-(cost - best) / lambda); when that average is worse than the best rollout (two equally good options, such as turning left or right, average to a bad one), the best rollout is the solution. Its first command is sent.

At the goal position (half the goal tolerance, or the tolerance once stopped), the robot turns in place to the goal's heading, braking in time; within both tolerances it stops. The tolerances are the strictest of this controller's and the system's (goal_tolerance.* in NavState).

Differential and holonomic robots

motion_model selects the robot:

  • diff: commands linear.x and angular.z. It always faces along the path.
  • omni: commands linear.x, linear.y and angular.z. The sequence is kept in the map frame, so turning does not change the course: the robot moves along the path and turns as heading_mode says, independently. Its translation is limited by the ellipse of max_linear_vel (min_linear_vel backwards) and max_lateral_vel: no direction is faster than the configured ones, diagonals included.

heading_mode sets the orientation of a holonomic robot while it moves (a differential robot always uses path). The goal's orientation is always reached at the end.

heading_mode Orientation while moving
path Facing the path ahead (heading_lookahead); it corrects sideways
free Any: the one that costs least
goal The goal's, from the start
hold The robot's when the goal was received
fixed heading_yaw (map frame)

While the NavState key heading_reference (double, rad, map frame) exists, a holonomic robot keeps that heading instead, whatever heading_mode says (e.g. set by a behavior tree).

Obstacles and the robot's shape

The obstacles are the perception points within obstacle_range of the robot, all around it, from z_min_filter to robot_geometry.height. Their distances are computed once per cycle in a grid (grid_resolution), so each rollout step looks them up in constant time.

With robot_geometry.footprint, the robot's polygon is checked, turned with each rollout: a long robot does not sweep a wall turning in place, and a holonomic one turns to cross a door narrower than its length. The radii of the footprint are used, not the configured ones (which may be planning margins). Without a footprint, the robot is a circle of robot_geometry.radius.
